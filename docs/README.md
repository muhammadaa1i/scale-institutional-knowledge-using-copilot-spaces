## Summary of the project management processes

OctoAcme runs cross-functional projects on a customer-first, iterative approach. Every project has a named Project Manager and Product Lead, and the work moves through five stages: initiation, planning, execution, release, and close with a retrospective. Each stage has its own guide and produces artifacts such as the project one-pager, the roadmap and release plan, the backlog with acceptance criteria, the risk register and retrospective notes.

During initiation and planning, the team defines the problem, stakeholders, scope, milestones and dependencies, and breaks work into backlog items and sprints. During execution, developers build, review and test in small increments, with QA validating the acceptance criteria. Progress is tracked through regular team rhythms and reported with simple delivery metrics.

Communication follows a fixed cadence: a weekly sync between the Project Manager and Product Manager, regular standups for the delivery team, monthly stakeholder updates and ad-hoc escalations. Risks are logged in a risk register and followed through their lifecycle, and communication templates keep stakeholder updates consistent.

Releases follow a pre-release checklist, a deployment checklist and a rollback and incident playbook. Each project closes with a retrospective whose action items are tracked, so lessons feed back into the process docs. The roles and personas document defines who is responsible for what across developers, product managers and project managers.
